# Why Data Science Matters

When I think about data science, I usually think about numbers, statistics, coding, and finding patterns in data. However, this course has helped me see that data science can also be used to understand people and society. Learning data science in social and interdisciplinary contexts is important because data is often connected to real people and real-world issues. It is not just numbers in a spreadsheet.

One reason I think it is valuable to model social topics and people is because data can help us better understand problems that are difficult to see otherwise. In sports analytics, for example, data can help us understand player performance, team decisions, and trends. The same ideas can be applied to topics outside of sports, such as education, economics, public health, and social behavior. Using data can help us identify patterns and ask better questions about why certain things happen.

At the same time, working with data about people comes with responsibilities. One of the biggest responsibilities is remembering that there are actual people behind the data. Data can be incomplete, inaccurate, or influenced by how it was collected. It is important to avoid making assumptions about individuals based only on numbers. Privacy is also important, especially when working with personal or sensitive information. I think data scientists have a responsibility to be honest about what their data can and cannot tell them.

Through this course, I hope to better understand how data science can be applied to different areas and how to approach social data responsibly. I also want to become better at interpreting data instead of simply producing results. As someone interested in sports analytics, I think these skills will be important because sports data is ultimately about people—athletes, coaches, fans, and organizations.

Overall, I want to learn how to use data not only to find answers, but also to ask better questions and understand the context behind those answers.
